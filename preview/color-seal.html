The seal is pressed, not printed. A registrar's seal, a notary's seal, a corporate seal — none of them carry ink; the mark is a deformation of the sheet. It needs paper beneath it, so it never appears on glass, on the aurora, or in chrome, and it is reserved for issued artifacts: the post-send confirmation, the Receipt, an envelope's postage corner. Oxblood was retired from the seal in August 2026 along with the rest of the system — a mark that appears once, on something already earned, should not be the heaviest object on the page.
