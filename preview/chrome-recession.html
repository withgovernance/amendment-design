The neutral ramp survives recession everywhere. `--text-primary` and `--text-secondary` are luminance against a low-chroma ground, so they clear 4.5:1 on the bare band in both schemes — which is what ink-needs-an-opaque-ground already said when it exempted primary/secondary text. The hard rule's "never bare text" is broader than its own reason.

Two things do not survive. The title over the brightest lobe keeps 14.03:1 with a ground and 6.22:1 without — it still passes, and it has lost 56% of its headroom to a gradient that moves. And the active item's Midnight Indigo, with no plate beneath it, measures 1.34:1 in dark: gone. That is primary.plate-not-mark arriving where it was always going to.

Recession does not make chrome quieter; it makes chrome absent. A surface with no material of its own cannot be still — it shows whatever moves under it, and the aurora breathes. So recession does not only remove a contrast ground, it removes the condition that makes "Chrome is still" expressible at all. The thing recession was reaching for — chrome that belongs to the page rather than sitting on top of a different one — was delivered on 2026-08-28 by giving chrome the canvas's own hue at the same opacity, which keeps the ground and costs nothing.
