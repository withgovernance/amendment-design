1. `DESIGN.md` — the canonical spec (~1,770 lines). Source of truth today.

3. `proposed/README.md` — a **prior session's restructuring proposal** (splits the monolith into `CLAUDE.md` + `design-system/` + `patterns/`). Do not redo the restructuring; do fold this memo's decisions into it.
4. `assets/README.md` — brand marks.

**Context you need:** Amendment is a legislative-intelligence product. Its thesis is that the rule of law is a living document and the people are its authors. The product's center of gravity moved from *understanding* (read a bill) to *acting* (write a letter, co-sign a demand, make a candidate answer on the record).

**The user.** An ordinary constituent. Arrives from a text message or a shared link, on a phone, with no prior civic literacy, and will leave in ninety seconds unless something holds them. Not a policy analyst. Not a lobbyist. The analyst is a happy secondary; the constituent is the one the product exists for.

**The implementation gap.** The production repo (`resistbot/voyager`) is private and has never been accessible from this project. Everything in `DESIGN.md` is spec-derived, not source-derived. Assume the spec and the codebase have drifted, and that some of what is documented was never built.

## 2. Do not touch these — they are the good part

Preserve verbatim, or preserve intent if reworded. A future session should feel actively discouraged from "improving" them.

- **The two-layer thesis** (ink beneath, breath above). It is generative — it produces the material tiers, the shadow rule, the motion posture, the paper-vs-glass rule. Most system concepts are decoration you could delete without changing a value. This one isn't.
- **"If the user reads or writes for more than thirty seconds, they need paper beneath the text."** A functional rule anyone can apply without asking.
- **One shadow ink, sourced from slate-900, never tinted by content category.**
- **The icon test** — "if the text label alone tells you what it is, the icon is decoration."
- **Motion discipline** — exactly two sanctioned surfaces, two named mechanics (Receipt *bloom* vs. Commons *accrual*), with an explicit rule that neither may borrow the other's moves. Best-specified part of the system.
- **The Heritage section** (Votizen, Designing Obama) — specifically the *"what is deliberately not carried forward"* list. Rare and valuable; it lets a new contributor make correct novel decisions.
- **The "What NOT to Do" list.** Consolidate it (per `proposed/`), don't dilute it.
- **The Paper Islands rule** — paper stays light in dark mode. Keep the rule; see §6 for the implementation concern.

## 3. Work items

### 3.1 — Add a newcomer register `[RESOLVED 2026-08 — the lede]`

> **Resolved, but not as prescribed below.** The "plain-language type role,
> explicitly *not* serif" was designed and set against real content on a phone
> (`explorations/The Newcomer.html`) — and it read flat: at one family and one
> weight, lede, facts and action all landed at the same value. Plainness had
> been assigned to the typeface, which then had no range left for hierarchy.
> What shipped is a **copy-and-hierarchy rule in the Archive's existing type**:
> `.role-lede` (one scale step), the one-sentence / three-facts / one-action
> structure, a jargon-replacement rule, and the thirty-second path. The
> statute-as-destination and the 30-second path landed as written below; the
> type role and the copy rules landed as one rule instead of two.
> See `DESIGN.md` → **The Newcomer's Lede** and `CHANGELOG.md`.

**The gap.** The system has a voice for institutions (Archive: clerical, exact, serif) and a voice for the crowd (Commons: present tense, live). It has **no voice for the person who has never read a bill.** Every default optimizes for gravitas and prolonged reading: serif body, 65ch columns, paper canvases, ceremonial receipts. The hardest user problem in civic tech is literacy, and it has no tokens, no pattern, and no layout.

**Build:**

- A **plain-language type role** (`body-plain` or similar) — sans, larger than body, looser leading, shorter measure (~45–55ch, not 65ch). Explicitly *not* serif. Serif is the register of the artifact; the explanation of the artifact should not cosplay as the artifact.
- A **"what this actually does" pattern** — the scan-first bill layout. Above the fold on a phone: what the bill does in one plain sentence, who it affects, what stage it's at, and one action. The statutory text is a *destination*, not the landing state.
- A defined **30-second path** as a first-class flow, documented alongside the five (soon three) surfaces.
- Copy rules for it in the Content Fundamentals section: short sentences, no legislative jargon without an inline gloss, no passive institutional voice. The existing tone signatures are all *institutional* or *ceremonial*; add one that is plainly helpful.

**Test to hold it to:** someone who has never heard the word "engrossed" should be able to act correctly without learning it.

### 3.2 — Find one ownable color `[HIGH]`

**The problem.** Every brand color is a Tailwind default, annotated as such in the file: Midnight Indigo = `slate-800`, Oxblood = `red-900`, Momentum = `emerald-700`, paper = `zinc`. Meanwhile the aurora — the "alive" half of the entire thesis — is constrained to 0.03–0.08 chroma so that opacity-based text stays contrast-safe, which makes it nearly imperceptible.

Net: the living layer is invisible and the ink layer is stock. The document writes beautifully about a chromatic identity the system does not actually possess. **Restraint is a choice among distinctive options; right now it is indistinguishable from not having chosen.**

**Do:**

- Pick **one** hue to own and author it in oklch as a real value, not a Tailwind alias. The strongest candidate is Oxblood — it already carries the most meaning (intent, the wax seal, the CTA) and a specific, slightly-warm, slightly-desaturated oxblood is defensible as *sealing wax* rather than *red-900*. Second candidate: the parchment. A specific paper white is quietly ownable and shows up on every Archive, Writing Desk, and Receipt surface.
- Keep slate and emerald as-is. One ownable color is enough; three would break the restraint that makes the system good.
- **Do not** solve this by broadening the aurora. The three-lobe / low-chroma constraint is load-bearing for text contrast and must survive.

**Deliverable:** a swatch exploration of 4–6 oxblood candidates against parchment and against the dark canvas, at CTA scale and at seal scale, before committing.

> Note also: this memo says twelve accents and then lists thirteen.

**The problem.** Twelve accents are documented: rubber stamp, wax seal, postmark, jurisdiction seal, ruled page, line numbers, signature line, folder-tab step, lapel-pin, progress ribbon, paper grain, envelope preview, guilloche. The jurisdiction seal — arguably the most evocative — is explicitly `status: Implementation removed; design TBD`. Documented-but-unbuilt is the worst state a component can be in: it generates false confidence and then five inconsistent one-off implementations.

**Define the promotion process** (it does not currently exist): an accent moves from proposed to canonical when it has (a) a named owner, (b) a shipped implementation, (c) a specimen in `preview/`, and (d) a stated communicative purpose passing the existing "ornament without meaning is forbidden" test.

**Note on guilloche:** it is the single most distinctive idea in the accent list and the one that most makes a Receipt feel *issued*. If any accent gets promoted early, promote this one — but only with a real implementation, not a spec paragraph.

### 3.5 — Collapse five surfaces to three `[RESOLVED 2026-08-02 — four, not three]`

> **Resolved at four.** Argued in `explorations/Five Rooms or Three.html` with
> both merges rendered side by side. The first held: Reading Room and Commons
> shared four of five tokens, and the fifth — motion posture — turned out to be
> a **contradiction** with `motion.rules` (which has always sanctioned exactly
> two moving surfaces) rather than a real distinction. Fixing it produced a
> rule the system was missing: **streaming is not motion.**
>
> The second merge did not hold. Writing Desk and Receipt differ on every
> token, and after §3.7 the Receipt carries a portrait HTML artifact *and* a
> three-ratio raster export family. A register that has to describe both is not
> one register; "compose, then issue" is good prose laid over a real geometry
> difference. **The Receipt is what the Writing Desk produces**, and a product
> is a different kind of thing from a workspace.
>
> Landed as a `registers:` block in `DESIGN.md` above `surfaces:`, with each
> surface carrying a `register:` key. Palette scoping moved from surface to
> condition: *green appears where a crowd is counted.*

**The problem.** Reading Room and Commons share material (`material-thin`), canvas (none), lead family (sans), and posture (on aurora, live). They differ in prose, not in tokens. Writing Desk and Receipt are the same room at two moments in time — compose, then issue.

**Proposed structure — three registers:**

| Register | Absorbs | Canvas | Material | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Live** (glass) | Reading Room, Commons | none — aurora | `material-thin` | accrual permitted |
| **Reading** (paper) | Archive | `paper-cream` | `material-regular` chrome around it | static |
| **Issued** (paper) | Writing Desk, Receipt | `paper-parchment` → `paper-cream` | `material-regular` chrome | bloom at issuance |

Keep the five room *names* as narrative in the thesis document if they're useful for talking to the team — they're good writing. But the **token-level system should have three**, so it is teachable in one sitting instead of requiring a document.

**Preserve through the collapse:** the bloom/accrual distinction (they now live in different registers, which actually clarifies it), and the paper-vs-glass functional rule.

### 3.6 — Kill the dual-calibrated weight scales `[MEDIUM]`

`typography.fontWeights` ships `sans-light` and `sans-dark` — nine paired values each (`normal: 362` vs `317`, etc.) to compensate for optical bloom in dark mode. The document itself says *"This is brittle but correct."*

It is brittle. It is not correct. It doubles every weight decision forever, the difference is imperceptible to users, and it will be silently abandoned the first time someone is in a hurry — leaving a system where half the components follow it and half don't, which is worse than either choice.

**Do:** collapse to one scale. If dark-mode bloom is genuinely a problem at display sizes, handle it with a single CSS-layer adjustment on one or two roles, not a parallel nine-step scale contributors must track. (`proposed/README.md` §4 already proposes moving this burden to the CSS layer — align with that.)

### 3.7 — Design mobile rather than assuming it `[MEDIUM]`

Breakpoints are Tailwind defaults with an explicit instruction not to write custom queries. The tab bar gets a single spec entry. The whole document reasons in desktop terms — columns, paper sheets, 65ch measures.

**Most concrete conflict:** the Receipt is described as *"a diploma issued by a phone"* and specified as **landscape, wider than tall, max-width 56rem.** A landscape certificate on a portrait phone is unresolved and it's the surface most likely to be screenshotted and shared.

**Do:**
- ~~Specify the Receipt's portrait behavior.~~ **Done 2026-08-02.** Resolved exactly as suggested: portrait HTML is the kept artifact, and the landscape proportion belongs only to the shared export — which is a rasterised image in **three** ratios (tall / square / wide), matching what production already ships. See `DESIGN.md` → *The Receipt is two artifacts*.
- ~~Give the tab bar a real spec (heights, safe-area, active state, label treatment at the corrected 12px floor).~~ **Done 2026-08-02**, and the framing above was off in an instructive way. *Label treatment at the corrected floor* implied a conflict to be managed; measured at 390pt there is none — the widest short label uses 72% of its slot at 12px. What the audit did not ask about is what was actually broken: **no safe-area inset** (labels overlap the home indicator by ~6px) and **tap targets sized by their content** (as narrow as 40px, against an `a11y.targetSize` rule that has said 44×44 all along and was never applied anywhere). Also adopted from production: the tab bar is a curated five requiring `shortLabel`, and **composing surfaces surrender the chrome** — the bar hides on `/conversation/` and `/action/`. See `DESIGN.md` → `components.tabbar` and `explorations/The Tab Bar at 390.html`.
- ~~State a mobile-first reading of every column-width token.~~ **Done 2026-08-04**, and the smallest thing left in the item turned out to be hiding the largest. Restating the tokens meant first asking what they measured, and `65ch` measured **97 characters**: `ch` is the advance width of the zero glyph *of the element carrying the rule*, and `.page--prose` inherits the body sans while the column holds serif prose. So the tokens are now stated `min(28rem, 100% - 2rem)` — narrow-first, no breakpoint — and `ch` is banned from length tokens. See `DESIGN.md` → `spacing.measure` and `explorations/What 65ch Measures.html`.

### 3.9 — Reconsider Merriweather `[RESOLVED 2026-08 — Newsreader]`

> **Resolved.** Replaced with **Newsreader**, a Scotch roman with a real
> optical-size axis. One correction to the finding below: Merriweather *is*
> variable on weight; what it lacks is an `opsz` axis, so a single drawing had
> to serve both display and statutory text. That, not the clot, was the
> deciding argument. Three optical stops now exist (`--serif-display`,
> `--serif-text`, `--serif-quote`), and serif italic has been narrowed to
> quoted voice at 18px and up. See `CHANGELOG.md`.

The thesis is *"document, not app."* Merriweather is a 2011 screen serif in the Google Fonts top 20 — it reads *blog*, and at weight 800 it clots at display sizes. Readex Pro with its HEXP axis is the system's genuine typographic differentiator; the serif isn't carrying comparable weight.

A transitional or Scotch face would say "statutory record" without being asked to, and would let the serif do work the prose is currently doing on its behalf.

**This is a large, expensive change and it is the item most likely to be wrong.** Do not act on it unilaterally. Present 3–4 alternatives in specimen alongside Merriweather at bill-title, section-head, and body-serif sizes, and let the human decide.

## 5. Standing constraints — do not violate while doing any of the above

- The aurora stays **three lobes, low chroma, monochromatic**. Text color is opacity on black/white; that invariant only holds because of this. No fourth lobe, no hue broadening.
- **No violet, pink, magenta, or fuchsia.** Ever. Removed deliberately; they read consumer-SaaS.
- **Amber is warning only.** Never a brand accent.
- **No nationalist iconography** — no flags, no eagles, no star-spangled ornament. Civic is not partisan.
- **No transform in hover states.** No `scale()`, no `filter: brightness()`.
- **Headings are never all-caps.** ALL CAPS belongs to HEXP-60 chrome alone.
- **Ornament without meaning is forbidden.** Every tactile accent must reference a real civic artifact *and* communicate something.
- Restraint is the resting state. The system marks real moments; it never manufactures one.

## 6. Two things flagged but not assigned

**The Paper Islands dark-mode variant.** The `@custom-variant dark (&:is(.dark *):not(.paper-cream, .paper-cream *, ...))` hack makes `dark:` utilities silently inert inside paper canvases. The *rule* is right — paper stays light in both modes. The *mechanism* is clever in a way that will confuse every new contributor, and silent inertness is a bad failure mode. Worth revisiting when someone touches the CSS layer; not worth a dedicated pass.

**Spec-to-source drift.** Everything here is derived from `DESIGN.md`, never from `resistbot/voyager`. Some of what is documented may not exist; some of what exists may not be documented. **If repo access becomes available, stop and reconcile before doing Phase 3** — the newcomer register and the color work should be built against what's real, not against the spec's account of it.

## 7. Tone note for whoever picks this up

The document's voice — calm, deliberate, opinionated, occasionally lyrical — is an asset and should survive every edit. The problem is not that it's well-written; it's that a designer asking "what's the card padding?" currently reads three paragraphs on civic register first. `proposed/` already solves that by splitting reference from manifesto. Keep the manifesto genuinely good. Make the reference genuinely fast. Don't let the compression flatten the conviction.
