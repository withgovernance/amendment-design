Ink and ground

Three rules that compose. The pair is necessary, the ground is necessary, and neither alone is sufficient. Every number here was measured on a rendered surface, and each names where it was taken.

1 — The switch is a property of the palette, not of green

Each token below is rendered as ink, on its own scheme's opaque ground. The base token holds on light and fails inverted; the -soft counterpart does the reverse. Not one token in the palette clears 4.5:1 in both schemes — which is why there is no such thing as "the" colour for a semantic meaning. There is a pair, and the scheme picks.

2 — Inside the aurora band, glass does not rescue a hue

Rendered inverted, because that is where these numbers were taken — the rule this card states forbids quoting a ratio without the scheme and position it came from, and an earlier draft of this very card showed light panels under dark figures. The three panels sit at the same position in the band and carry the same ink. Only the ground differs. Glass is translucent, so the gradient passes through it; and blur(50px) samples a wide neighbourhood and averages neighbouring bright lobes in — so a glass panel near a lobe is lighter than the canvas beneath it. Ordering the materials by thickness does not help: every tier lands 2.6–3.0:1, with the thickest among the worst.

Glass · blurred Co-signed 2.87:1 ✗
Thicker glass Co-signed 3.38:1 ✗
Opaque · --canvas Co-signed 6.10:1 ✓

Coloured ink requires an opaque ground. Glass stays legal for primary and secondary text, which is luminance against its own material and does not depend on hue separation. This retired the rule that said material-chrome "re-establishes a ground" — it does not, and that rule had never been rendered.

3 — And the ground itself moves

The band is a gradient, so a token's contrast is a function of where the element sits. The strip below is one continuous aurora with the same ink at five heights — nothing changes but position. Measured on the shipped dark page: 2.15:1 at the top of the band, rising to 6.32:1 below it. A threefold swing with no change to token, scheme, or material.

Two sessions measured the same token on the same page and disagreed by a factor of two. Both were right for where they sampled.

A contrast number without a position is not a measurement. That is why this card carries no summary table of "the" contrast for each token, and why the spec carries none either — it would be a false constant. Quote the position or do not quote the number. The opaque ground is not uniformly safer, either: it is darker than glass in light and lighter in dark, so requiring it moves every token in opposite directions by scheme. Danger was the first token off that edge, which is why it moved from red-600 to red-700.
