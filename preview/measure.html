The measure

A measure caps the element that holds the text, and it is stated in rem derived from a counted line. The count is the intent; the rem is the value; and a count belongs to the prose it was taken on, not to the width.

was: 65ch on the page shell… · first line … · lines …

I rent in your district and my lease went up eleven percent this spring, which is the third increase in four years and more than my pay has moved in that time.

measure.body · 28rem… · first line … · lines …

I rent in your district and my lease went up eleven percent this spring, which is the third increase in four years and more than my pay has moved in that time.

measure.lede · 24rem… · first line … · lines …

Your landlord could raise the rent by any amount, with sixty days’ notice.

The unit
ch is banned from length tokens, and from prose that states a length. It is the advance width of the zero glyph of the element carrying the rule — so 65ch on a shell that inherits the body sans, wrapping serif prose, computes … here and sets this sample’s first line at … characters. Nobody chose that number.
Two ambiguities
Zero vs average glyph within a face: Newsreader’s zero is … wider than this sample’s average character. Measuring face vs rendered face between two: the sans zero is … wider than Newsreader’s. Self-consistency fixes only the second: 65ch set on the serif itself still sets … characters on this sample’s first line.
Where it goes
On the text, not the shell. A page may be wide; the paragraph is capped. container.* are shells, measure.* are measures.
The counts
A count is a line of specific prose, not a property of a width. measure.body and measure.lede each set a band, lowest on statutory prose with its long words — body-count-is-a-band, lede-count-is-a-band; the target and ceiling are measure.range. The min() in both tokens is why neither needs a breakpoint. Metadata grids, rolls and tallies are not copy and may run wider.
On paper
6.5in, set by the 1in margin — paper takes its measure from the margin, not from a count. See profilesPrint.
