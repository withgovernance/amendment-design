Texas · 89th Legislature

HB 21 — Relating to public access to legislative records

Introduced version · as filed
Section 1 · Short title

This Act may be cited as the Legislative Records Access Act. The provisions of this Act apply to every record produced in the course of a committee proceeding, including records held by a subcommittee acting under delegated authority.

Section 2 · Definitions

In this Act, record means any document, recording, or data set created, received, or maintained by a legislative body, without regard to the medium in which it is fixed. Proceeding means a hearing, markup, or vote noticed under the rules of either chamber.

A record does not lose its character as a record because it is held by a contractor, a vendor, or an agent acting on behalf of a legislative body.

Section 3 · Publication

Not later than the seventh day after a proceeding concludes, the presiding officer shall cause each record of that proceeding to be published in a machine-readable format at no charge. Publication under this section does not require a request.

The presiding officer may withhold a record only on a ground enumerated in Section 5, and shall publish, with the remaining records, a written statement of the ground relied on.

Section 4 · Format

A record published under Section 3 must be published in a format that permits automated extraction of its text. A scanned image of a printed page does not satisfy this requirement unless it is accompanied by an accurate text layer.

Where a record is a recording of a proceeding, the presiding officer shall publish a transcript not later than the thirtieth day after the proceeding concludes.

Section 5 · Grounds for withholding

A record may be withheld if its publication would disclose the identity of a person who has requested confidentiality under a program established by statute, or if publication is prohibited by an order of a court of competent jurisdiction.

The grounds in this section are exclusive. A ground not enumerated here may not be relied on, and the burden of establishing a ground rests with the officer asserting it.

Section 6 · Effective date

This Act takes effect September 1 of the year in which it is enacted, except that Section 4 takes effect January 1 of the following year.

Why this is the target and not a swatch.
The two sheets differ by one declaration. A specimen card renders them identically, a screenshot diff passes, and the regression ships. background-attachment is the kind of property that gets dropped by a well-meaning refactor — it looks redundant next to no-repeat, and Tailwind has no arbitrary utility for a per-layer list, so it has to survive as raw CSS in the .dark .paper-cream block.
Also load-bearing here: the @custom-variant dark at the top of globals.css excludes .paper-cream, .paper-parchment and their descendants, which is why ink on both sheets is rgb(0 0 0/87%) in a dark room. Paper is a light island. A refactor that "simplifies" that variant back to plain &:is(.dark *) inverts every glyph on the page — and fails silently, since nothing errors. This design system deliberately does not copy that mechanism: it marks the canvas [data-paper] so the opt-out is explicit. That is a spec-ahead improvement, not a divergence to reconcile backwards.
Parchment differs only in temperature — oklch(0.77 0.067 64) against cream's oklch(0.78 0.058 68), pools at hue 76/74 instead of 80/78, inset ring at 0.55 alpha instead of 0.5. Letterhead stock is warmer than archive parchment. Same lamp geometry, so the same target applies.
Status in this project: already landed. colors_and_type.css carries these values as --paper-cream / --paper-parchment plus --paper-*-lamp, and the near-black oklch(.20 .010 70) RECONCILIATION §4 flagged is gone. The remaining work is in the repo, not here.
