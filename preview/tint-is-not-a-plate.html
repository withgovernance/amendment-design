A tint is not a plate

The fill rule says a filled green plate is a control. It says nothing about alpha — so the open question was where a tint stops being a chip and starts being fill. The render answers it differently than the question assumed: alpha is not the channel carrying the affordance at all.

Four channels the tint never touches

The control's identity does not ride on how green it is. It rides on these, and no amount of alpha on a flat pill reproduces any of them.

FillFull token strength, not a tint of it.
ElevationA raised shadow tier. The chip is flat.
RadiusA control is rounded-md; a chip is rounded-full.
LabelInk on the plate, not coloured ink on a ground.

The photometry agrees

Measured as share of the CTA's own separation from its ground, the ladder reaches at most 30% in light and 17% inverted. A tint never approaches the control at any alpha. So the question "at what alpha does tint become fill" has no answer — not because it is hard, but because it is the wrong axis.

Status is ink on neutral material; a coloured plate belongs to a control. That answers the general case as well as the green one — there is no tint token in any family and there should not be one. The 10% ceiling still binds, and it binds for contrast, not affordance: at 10% the ink measures 4.52:1 on the page ground, clearing by two hundredths, and at 15% it is 4.31:1 and fails. That 10% is a framework step nobody chose — it survives on measurement, not intent, and it has no room to grow. Which is the whole reason to write it down before someone rounds it up to a nicer number.
